An interactive front end to a Bayesian Bliss model of phage-phage interaction.
It takes either a single phage pair typed by hand or a full screening table, and it is deliberately agnostic about the naming, layout and size of that table.

## App Workflow

### **1. Two types of Inputs**

**Quick pair.** Type a strain name, two phage names, and the effect of phage A alone, phage B alone and the combination. Each field accepts one value or
several replicates separated by commas or spaces. Useful for a single experiment, or for checking one pair from a paper.

**Data table.** Upload (`.csv`, `.tsv`, `.txt`, `.xlsx`, `.xls`, `.rds`, `.RData`), paste a block copied from a spreadsheet, or load the built-in
simulated example (also downloadable as a template). Excel workbooks and `.RData` files offer a sheet/object picker.

The example is generated by `make_mock_data()` in `app/logic/mock_data.R`: 8 strains x 3 replicates, three phages (`PhiA`, `PhiB`, `PhiC`), their three pairs, the triple and a `Nophage` control. Strains 01-03 are built with a positive deviation from Bliss (synergy), 04-06 with none (additivity) and 07-08 with a negative one (antagonism), so the app's output can be checked against a known answer.

Both paths go through the same engine, so the arithmetic, the threshold and the model are identical whichever one is used.


### **2. Outputs**

Both figures are interactive: hover any point for its numbers, drag to zoom, double-click to reset, and use the expand icon at the bottom right of the card to fill the screen. They are also sized from the data rather than squeezed into a fixed box, so a 25-strain screen gets 25 readable rows and the card scrolls.

- **Interaction map** - bubble matrix of strains against combinations, bubble size = observed combination effect, fill = interaction, on a divergent scale centred on zero. Choose what the colour shows (observed gap, posterior synergy effect, or the posterior probability of synergy) and whether strains are ordered alphabetically or by interaction strength. A single pair gets an effect-ladder plot instead.
- **Results** - one row per strain and combination: observed mean, Bliss expectation, gap, and, once the model is fitted, the posterior median, its 95% credible interval, the posterior probabilities, the Bayes factor and the call. Sortable, with a filter box on every column.
- **Posterior** - forest plot of the synergy effect against the region of practical equivalence, with controls for which combinations to show, how many panels per row and how strains are ordered, plus the convergence table (R-hat, effective sample size).
- **Data** - the prepared table, with a note on what was detected, rescaled or clamped.
- **CSV / PNG export** - the results table, and whichever figure is on screen at a height matched to its contents.

### **3. Decision rule**

A gap between the observed combination effect and the Bliss expectation is only called an interaction when it exceeds the _region of practical equivalence_(ROPE), by default the median within-strain replicate standard deviation with a
floor of 0.05. A difference smaller than the assay's own noise is not an interaction. The Bayesian call additionally requires the posterior to be at least 95% (adjustable) sure of the category, otherwise the pair stays _Inconclusive_ rather than being forced into one.

## What "agnostic" means here

- **No fixed column names.** The strain and replicate columns are guessed from the header and can be overridden. Both are optional: without a strain column every row belongs to one sample; without a replicate column each row within a strain is treated as one replicate.
- **No fixed treatment names.** A column is read as a _combination_ when its name splits into components that are themselves columns. `V1SA12+V1SA19`, `p1_p2`, `phageA:phageB` and `A|B` all work; the separator is detected from the header and can be overridden.
- **Any number of phages per combination.** `P1+P2+P3` is analysed exactly like a pair, through the generalised Bliss expectation.
- **Controls fall out on their own.** `Nophage`, `all_phages`, `control`, `blank` and friends are pre-selected in the "columns to ignore" list, and any
  column whose parts do not match single treatments is never read as a combination.
- **Wide or long.** One column per treatment, or one row per measurement with treatment/value columns.
- **Either scale.** Effects are fractions of growth suppressed; a table on a 0-100 scale is detected and divided by 100.

## Model (Bayesian Bliss)

The app fits `bliss_zhao_n`, the replicate-level Bliss model generalised to $n_p$ phages. For each bacterium $b$, each phage $k$ of the combination and each replicate $r$:

- Single-phage effects are modelled on the probability scale through a logit-normal hierarchy:

$$
logit(p_{k,b}) \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 0.16)
$$

- The Bliss expected effect is the complement of the survival product:

$$
\mu_b = 1 - \prod_{k=1}^{n_p} (1 - p_{k,b})
$$

which for $n_p = 2$ is the familiar $p_{1,b} + p_{2,b} - p_{1,b}p_{2,b}$.

- A bacteria-specific deviation from Bliss is added on the logit scale:

$$
\delta_b \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \tau_{\delta}), \quad
logit(p_{c,b}) = logit(\mu_b) + \delta_b
$$

- The interaction effect reported by the model is:

$$
\mathrm{synergy}_b = p_{c,b} - \mu_b
$$

- Observation model for replicates:

$$
P_{k,b,r} \sim \text{Beta}(p_{k,b}\phi_s, (1-p_{k,b})\phi_s),
$$

$$
Y_{obs,b,r} \sim \text{Beta}(p_{c,b}\phi_c, (1-p_{c,b})\phi_c)
$$

- Main priors:

$$
\sigma_\delta \sim \mathcal{U}(0,2), \quad
tau_{\delta} = \sigma_\delta^{-2}, \quad
\phi_s \sim \Gamma(2,0.1), \quad
\phi_c \sim \Gamma(2,0.1)
$$

Interpretation:

- $\mathrm{synergy}_b > 0$: observed combination tends to be stronger than Bliss expectation (synergy)
- $\mathrm{synergy}_b < 0$: observed combination tends to be weaker than Bliss expectation (antagonism)

The Bayes factor divides the posterior odds of a relevant synergy by the prior odds obtained by simulating the same priors forward, so the evidence is reported separately from the prior.
